# Meet your recorder

::pip "Hi, I'm Pip! I'll help you learn the recorder, one note at a time."

Most recorders come apart in two or three pieces: the **head** with the mouthpiece, the **body** with seven holes on the front and one on the back, and often a separate **foot** at the bottom.

You blow into the mouthpiece. The air hits a sharp edge, the **labium**, and that makes the sound. Covering and uncovering the holes changes the note.

## Holding it

- Your **left hand** goes on top, your right hand below.
- Your left **thumb** covers the hole on the back.
- Hold the mouthpiece lightly between your lips, not your teeth.
- Sit or stand up straight and keep your shoulders relaxed.

## Blowing

Blow very gently, as if you were breathing on cold hands. Start every note with your tongue, as if you whisper **"du"**. Blowing too hard makes a squeak; that happens to everyone at first.

## Your first note: Si

Cover the back hole with your thumb and the first hole on the front with your left index finger. Press with the soft part of your fingers, so no air leaks out.

::fingering note=B4
